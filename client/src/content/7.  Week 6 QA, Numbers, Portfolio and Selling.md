# 📅 Week 6: QA, Numbers, Portfolio and Selling (full step-by-step guide)

# **Week goal:**

a fully tested store, baseline performance numbers saved for Stage 4, a finished portfolio (screenshots, Loom video, case study) and a sellable offer with packages, a process and outreach messages. **By Day 7 you contact your first 10 prospects.**

**Time needed:** about 1.5–2 hours a day for 7 days.

I checked this against Shopify’s Help Center, the Dev Dashboard docs, Chrome’s Lighthouse docs and current Loom pricing. **Six corrections to what I told you earlier:**

- **The “Bogus Gateway” is now called the Test payment gateway** (same card numbers: `1`, `2`, `3`).
- **PageSpeed Insights can’t test your store.** Dev stores are always password-protected, so external tools only see the password page. **Use Lighthouse in Chrome DevTools**, with **“Clear storage” turned off** so it keeps your password session.
- **The admin’s web performance reports will stay empty.** They use real visitor data and need password protection turned off, which dev stores don’t allow. **Lighthouse (lab) numbers are your baseline.**
- **Real client stores must be client transfer stores**, not dev stores. There’s a bonus too: when a client activates a paid plan through your transfer, Shopify pays you **20% of their monthly subscription plus 0.1% of eligible online sales, for 4 years**.
- **The Shopify Partner Directory isn’t open to beginners.** Listings are for Plus tier and above (it needs things like 40 standard merchant stores). **Don’t count on it for early clients.**
- **Loom’s free plan allows 5-minute recordings and up to 25 videos**, so a 2–3 minute walkthrough fits.

# 🟢 Day 3: Baseline Performance Numbers

## 3.1 Why Lighthouse and not PageSpeed Insights

| Tool | Works on SOLENNE? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PageSpeed Insights** | ❌ | Only sees the password page |
| **Admin web performance reports** | ❌ Stays empty | Needs real visitors with password protection off, which dev stores can’t do |
| **Sidekick “show web performance”** | ⚠️ No data | Same reason |
| **Lighthouse in Chrome DevTools** | ✅ | Runs in your browser, using your password session |

**Lab vs. field data:** Lighthouse measures **one simulated visit** (lab data). The admin reports measure **real visitors** (field data). **For a client’s live store in Stage 4, you’ll use both.**

## 3.2 How to run Lighthouse correctly

1. Open Chrome **in incognito** (extensions can slow pages down and distort the results)
2. Go to the store → **enter the password**
3. Open the page you want to test
4. **DevTools** (F12) → the **Lighthouse** tab
5. **Mode:** Navigation · **Device:** Mobile · **Categories:** Performance, Accessibility, Best practices, SEO
6. Click the **gear icon (settings)** → **turn off “Clear storage.”** If it stays on, Lighthouse clears your password session and tests the password page instead.
7. **Analyze page load**
8. **Run it 3 times** and **use the middle (median) result**, because scores vary from run to run
9. Repeat for **Desktop**

**⚠️ Because storage isn’t cleared, results can be slightly better than a first-time visit.** That’s fine, **as long as you use the same method in Stage 4**, so before and after compare fairly.

**About the metrics:**

- **LCP** (loading): the target is **2.5s or less**
- **CLS** (visual stability): the target is **0.1 or less**
- **TBT** (Total Blocking Time) stands in for **INP**, because Lighthouse can’t measure real interactions. INP (target **200ms or less**) comes from real-visitor data in Stage 4.

**Reference targets:** Shopify’s Theme Store expects an **average performance score of at least 60** across the home, product and collection pages, and **90+ for accessibility.** **If you’re below that, note it now.** It becomes a Stage 4 task, not a reason to delay this week.

### **SOLENNE: a calm, premium skincare store on Shopify**

*Concept project · Store design · Shopify Horizon (Ritual) · [Month 2026]*

**🔗 Live demo:** [store URL] · Password: [password]

**🎥 Walkthrough (3 min):** [Loom link]

---

**The brief**

Design a store for a fictional botanical skincare brand. It needed to feel calm and premium, be easy for a small team to update, and help shoppers pick the right routine.

**The challenge**

Skincare shoppers need a lot of information (ingredients, skin type, how to use) before they buy. Most small stores either hide it or cram it in. The store also needed to stay consistent as products are added.

**Key decisions**

- **An accessible palette.** The brand’s sage and terracotta failed contrast tests for text (about 2.7:1 and 2.9:1). I added darker “text-safe” versions (about 5:1) and kept the originals for decoration only.
- **Two fonts, four roles.** Cormorant Garamond for headings and DM Sans for body text: elegant without slowing the site down.
- **One product template.** All product details come from 6 structured fields (metafields), connected with dynamic sources, so every product page builds itself.
- **Shop by concern.** Shoppers think in problems (“my skin feels dry”), not categories, so navigation and collections are built around concerns as well as product types.
- **Honest trust.** No invented reviews. Trust badges, clear policies and consistent promises instead.

**What I built**

- A brand system: an 8-color palette, type scale, buttons, spacing and a style guide
- Homepage, product, bundle and collection templates, plus About, FAQ and Contact
- **7 automated collections** that fill themselves using rules
- A native **fixed bundle**, with stock tracking tested
- **Filters** (skin type, size, price), **5 synonym groups** and **4 search boosts**
- Policies, return rules and a checkout tested with the Test payment gateway

**How I used AI (and where I didn’t)**

| AI did | I did |

| First drafts of product copy and the FAQ (Shopify Magic, Sidekick) | Rewrote everything in the brand voice and removed medical claims |
| Product and lifestyle imagery (gpt-image-2) | Art direction, consistency checks, compression and alt text |

| — | Architecture, the brand system, data structure, QA and accessibility |

## 6.3 Pricing honestly

- **Market reality:** Upwork lists a **median Shopify developer rate of about $20/hr**, and typical **theme customization projects at $500–$2,000**

## 6.5 How client projects work in Shopify

| Situation | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| **New store (the client has no Shopify store yet)** | Create a **client transfer store** in the Dev Dashboard → build it → **Transfer store** → the client accepts (**the invitation expires after 7 days**) and picks a plan. You keep collaborator access. |
| **Existing store** | Send a **collaborator request** from the Dev Dashboard. **Ask the client for their collaborator request code** if their store requires one. |
| **Work on an existing live store** | **Duplicate the live theme** and work on the copy. **Never edit the live theme directly.** |

**Client transfer store facts:**

- The store name sets the `myshopify.com` URL, and **it can’t be changed later**, so use the client’s brand name
- **Only free and partner-friendly apps** can be installed before the transfer
- **Deactivate Shopify Payments** (and other Shopify financial products) before transferring, or the transfer fails
- **Add the client’s address** and check the billing currency before transferring
- **Partner earnings:** if the client activates their plan through your transfer, Shopify pays you **20% of their monthly subscription + 0.1% of eligible online sales, for 4 years.** Shopify can change these terms, but it’s still good passive income to mention to yourself, not to the client.

## 6.6 The discovery call (20–30 minutes)

**Before the call:** look at their current store or Instagram, and write down 3 things you’d improve.

**The 10 questions (with the reason for each):**

| # | Question | Why you ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Who is your customer, and **what do they worry about before buying?** | Shapes the product page and trust elements |
| 2 | How many **products and variants**, and **where is the data now** (spreadsheet, another platform)? | Scope and timeline |
| 3 | Do you have **good product photos**? | Photos are the biggest risk to the timeline |
| 4 | Do you have a **logo, colors and fonts**? | Whether a brand system is needed from scratch |
| 5 | Which **2–3 stores do you like, and why?** | Visual direction |
| 6 | What’s the **main goal**: sales, email sign-ups or brand trust? | What the homepage focuses on |
| 7 | **Who updates the store** after launch? | How editable it needs to be (sells Standard/Premium) |
| 8 | Do you sell **bundles, subscriptions or pre-orders**? | Bundles don’t work with subscriptions or pre-orders |
| 9 | **When do you want to launch**, and is there a fixed date? | Timeline and urgency |
| 10 | **What budget range** did you have in mind? | Which package fits |

**End of the call:** *“Thanks. I’ll send you a short proposal with a recommended package within 24 hours.”*
